**Title:** I made a 69-min film that tests three "extraordinary" claims fairly — and never says "proven"

**Body:**
I've been bothered for a while by how we handle bold or fringe ideas: we either worship them or we
mock them, and neither actually tests anything. So I built a small reproducible "math lab" and made
a film around one question — *what would it look like to honor a hopeful idea and then test it
honestly, all the way down?*

It takes three claims (ozone as the source of life; weight as air pressure instead of gravity; a
single thread through all living things), decomposes each into its separate testable readings, and
labels every result with an evidence class. Some survive, some are contradicted (the pressure-vs-
gravity one fails hard on seven worlds), some are preserved as metaphor. Every number is re-derived
from public NASA/ESA/JPL data by open code, and the word "proven" is used nowhere.

I'd genuinely like feedback on the *method* — especially from people who think about demarcation and
fair testing of unconventional claims. Happy to share the full thing if it's allowed here; mods,
tell me the right way. (Captions in English and Marathi.)
