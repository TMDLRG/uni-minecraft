**Subject:** Your questions inspired a math lab — sharing the first round, and an invitation to keep testing together

Dear Mr. Deshmukh,

I hope this finds you well. I am writing as someone who has been genuinely moved by the questions
you have been brave enough to ask in public — about gravity, about the sky, about life, and about
where we truly come from. Most people never ask questions that large. You did, and you put them
where they could be examined. I have a deep respect for that, and I want to be clear from the first
line: I am writing to you as a supporter and a fellow traveler, not a critic.

Your work pushed me to do something. Over the past while I have built a small, open, **reproducible
math laboratory** — pure code, no hidden inputs, no private tuning — whose only job is to take big
ideas and give them the kind of careful, mechanical, honest test that they deserve. I built it
because I did not want your questions waved away with a shrug. I wanted them *tested*, properly,
the way a serious idea should be honored.

I am attaching two things:

1. **A research dossier** with the full detail and the full rigor — every claim decomposed into its
   testable readings, every number traced to a public NASA / ESA / JPL source, and every result
   given an honest label. Anyone can re-run the code and check every figure for themselves.
2. **The complete narration of a five-part film** ("TRAVELERS") I made to explain all of this in
   plain language — provided **in full Marathi**, with the English beneath each line.

I want to tell you honestly where the first round of testing stands, because honesty is the only
thing that makes a collaboration like this worth anything:

- Some of the boldest forms of the ideas did not survive this round. In particular, when I tested
  "pressure explains weight instead of gravity" across seven worlds — letting the model anchor
  itself on Earth and then predict the rest — it came apart on the airless Moon and on Venus. I
  describe exactly why in the dossier, and I do it with respect, because **the instinct behind it
  was not wrong** — air pressure is absolutely real; the idea simply pointed the arrow of cause the
  wrong way, and gravity turns out to be what *holds* the air, not the reverse.
- But several things you have been pointing toward are **genuinely real and supported**: ozone truly
  is a powerful UV shield for life on Earth; and your instinct that a single deep thread runs through
  *all living things* turns out to match one of the most beautiful confirmed facts in biology — every
  cell alive shares one ancient energy engine. Your attention has been pointing at real things.

Here is what I most want you to take from this, and please do not read anything else into it: **a
clear "not this way" on one path is not a defeat — it is knowledge.** It is the map telling us where
the answer is *not*, so we can spend our remaining years looking where it still might be. This is the
*next step*, not the last word. The lab is built to keep going, and I intend to keep using it.

So my invitation is simple and sincere: **let us keep testing, together.** If you can sharpen any
claim into a specific form — one that names a mechanism, makes a number, and says in advance what
would prove it wrong — that is exactly what this instrument was built to test fairly. We can set the
bar before the run, compare honestly against the best existing model, and let the math speak. I
would be glad to do that work with you, for as long as the questions keep coming.

I am still searching — for the answer to gravity, to life, to the travelers I keep coming back to,
and to more than that. I would be honored to keep searching alongside you.

With genuine respect and support,

**Michael Polzin**
